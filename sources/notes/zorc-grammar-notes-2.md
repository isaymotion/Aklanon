## 1. Morphophonemics (pp. 39–47; context pp. 38, 48)

**Summary**
- Roots come in a *normal* (full) form and a *reduced/changed* form that appears after affixation. For example, *bakae* → *bake-* in *bakean*, *bakeon*. The book counts **ten types** of change (p. 39).
- Aklanon tends to keep the stress on the penultimate syllable and to keep words to two syllables. Suffixes therefore shift stress and often delete or glide the last root vowel.
- *ng* in a prefix (pang-, mang-, sang-, kasing-) **assimilates**: it becomes *m* before b/p and *n* before d/t. It stays *ng* before k, g, e, h, glottal stop, w and y.
- After assimilation, the root's first consonant is often **lost**: p/b after *m*; d/t/s after *n*; k and the glottal stop after *ng*. This is how the distributive *pang-/mang-* forms are built (see §4).
- *d* tends to become *r* at the end of a root before a suffix, and occasionally at the start of a root after a prefix. The *e*-sound becomes *l* next to the *-in-* infix. In fast speech, *eon* is pronounced *yun* after t, d, s, n and y.
- Spoken contractions are pervasive: *akon nga* → *akong*; *ro akon nga* → *rang*.
- Reduplication marks diminution or artificiality (*baeay-baeay*), intensification (*-in-*, *-eV-*) and lessened likelihood (CV-). "Primitive words" are old two-syllable roots made of a repeated syllable.

## 2. Tense, the conditioned and participle forms, verb stem classes, and the inflection chart (pp. 59–65, 90–98)

**Summary**
- Verbs mark **aspect**, not clock time. Explicit time comes from adverbs and particles (pp. 59, 65).
  - *na-* = **real** aspect: the action has begun.
  - *ma-* = **unreal** aspect: the action has not begun.
  - With *owa'* a form refers to the past; with *indi'* it refers to the future (p. 61).
- **Real tenses**:
  - imperfective *naga-*: present, habitual, or ongoing in the past;
  - perfective *nag-*: simple past or pluperfect;
  - past conditioned *mag-*.
- **Unreal tenses**:
  - expected *manog-*: "about to" or "intends to";
  - future *maga-*;
  - subjunctive *ma-* (alternate *-um-*): after *mos* "come on", and after *basi'*, *kunta'*, *basta*, *bago*, *kon*, *siguro*, etc.;
  - dependent *mag-*/*ga-*: like an English infinitive, after *naila'*, *buot*, *antigo*, *kinahangean*, *dapat*, *gusto*;
  - future conditioned *mag-* (alternate *-um-*).
- **Participle *pag-***: a gerund ("the reading and writing…"), or past time in a dependent clause (*Pag-abot ni Tatay, …* "When Father arrived, …").
- **Past conditioned** is used after *owa'*, *kan-o*, *isot* ("almost did") and *siin* (past), and also after *ayaw*. **Future conditioned** is used after *indi'*. The two can alternate freely, especially with question words (pp. 64–65).
- The book says a separate *-um-* conjugation "does not exist in current Aklanon". *-um-* survives only as a subjunctive, imperative or conditioned alternate (p. 63).
- Not every root takes every focus. Which focuses a root takes depends on its **stem class** (I–IX, pp. 90–92). The book borrows this scheme from Reid's Ivatan grammar and calls it provisional.
- The book's own tags (Appendix 1, p. 212) are a quality letter plus focus, mode and tense:
  - quality: g = general, c = causative, d = distributive;
  - focus: AF, IF, OF, RF;
  - mode: S = simple, A = ability, H = happenstance, I = imperative;
  - tense: impf, perf, conp (past conditioned), conf (future conditioned), expt, futr, subj, depn, part, comm, real, unrl.
  - For example, *masimba* = gAF-S/subj (p. 98).
  - Other tags: ST1, ST2… = Stative Verb Class 1, 2…; ST-Q = stative quality; REL/ = mag- relationship class; RECIP/ = makig- class; BECOM/ = maging- class; DISTR/ = mang- class.

**Dictionary tags.** The grammar itself never uses RV-, CV- or DV-tags. Vol. 2, the dictionary (OCR `zorc/dict-ocr.txt`, pp. 25–27), gives a revised scheme:
- **RV1–RV9** = Regular Verb Classes 1–9:
  - RV1: transitive; Object Focus #1 (bakeon / bakea);
  - RV2: goal shown by Instrument Focus (ibaligya' / baligya-an);
  - RV3: goal shown by it or sa, with -an/-i (eabhan / eabhi);
  - RV4: goal shown only with sa (lingkoran, sakyan);
  - RV5: object bound into the verb (most *pang-* verbs);
  - RV6: intransitive motion;
  - RV7: inanimate, no pronoun or *si* actor (uean, ilog);
  - RV8: always plural doers (saeabtanan, kasundo');
  - RV9: *pa-* directional motion (paKalibo).
- **CV** = causative *pa-* verb. It has one conjugation: AF = causer, IF = object or instrument, OF = agent, RF = referents.
- **DV** = distributive *pang-* verb.
- **ST1–ST5** = stative classes:
  - ST1: feelings, conjugated like OF #1 but with actor meaning (gutom, duyog, uhaw);
  - ST2: feelings and physical conditions, na-/hi-/ha- forms only (akig, hadlok, matay);
  - ST3: inanimate accidental happenings (buka', gisi', samad);
  - ST4: physical afflictions of the body (sakit, piling, sip-on);
  - ST5: psychological reaction to an outside phenomenon, conjugated like RF (Naeayo-an ako ku biyahi).

## 3. Roles and focus (pp. 70–89)

**Summary**
- An action sits among **situational roles**: actor, instrument (associate), companion or concomitant, direct object (goal), indirect object or beneficiary, place and time. Aklanon groups them into **four grammatical roles, the focuses** (p. 74).
- **Focus is "the grammatical relationship signalled by the verbal affix to a certain phrase"** (p. 75). The focused phrase becomes the **topic**, marked by ro/do, a topic pronoun, si/sanday or a topic deictic. Only one focus is allowed per clause.
- **What each focus emphasises** (pp. 80–82):
  - **Actor**: the doer.
  - **Instrument**: an instrument, companion, conveyed or moved object, the content of speech, a special beneficiary, a future time, or a reason.
  - **Object**: a goal that receives the *whole* action.
  - **Referent**: a place, indirect object, beneficiary, partitive object (one only partly affected), person affected, indirect cause, or a noun or adjective used as a verb.
- **Non-topic markers**: ku = an actor that is not in focus, or an instrument or associate; it = the object when not in Object Focus; sa = referents (para sa = beneficiary).
- Actor and Instrument are "subjective" focuses, mostly prefixes. Object and Referent are "objective" focuses, with prefixes plus suffixes (p. 93).
- **Focus is not voice.** An Object Focus sentence with no actor *indefinitises* the actor rather than hiding it. Even with *ni George* added, the stress stays on the object. Everyday idioms that English puts in the active voice are oblique-focus in Aklanon, and the Actor Focus versions are "incorrect idioms" (pp. 83–86).
- **"Use of focus is a matter of style"** (p. 87): put in focus whatever the speaker feels is important. Overusing Actor Focus sounds childish or vain, and natural conversation switches focus often (p. 88).
- **"A command of focus is crucial"** (p. 89): focus is "the most important point in the whole structure of the language". Learning only Actor or Object Focus "is not enough". Radio speakers on Good Friday 1968 switched focus on the fly for effect.

## 4. Distributive and other verb classes; other verb qualifiers (pp. 106–119)

**Summary**
- **Distributive *pang-*** (always with the §1 assimilation and loss) spreads the action:
  - over time or over many objects (*pamakae* "buy plenty");
  - into a process idiom (*panguto* "take lice from hair");
  - or builds the goal into the verb (*pangisda'* "go fishing").
- Roles and focus stay the same as in general quality (p. 106).
- The *pang-* **instrumental gerund** is a different, homophonous prefix with no sound change: *pangsimba* is "church clothes", while *panimba* is "going to church" (p. 108).
- **mang- class** (p. 109): *nang-* (real) and *mang-* (unreal) are the only forms, and they are always Actor Focus. Nouns of occupation add a nasal + vowel: *mangingisda'* ("fisherman").
- ***maging-*** (p. 110): *nagin(g)-* (real) and *magin(g)-* (unreal) on nouns or pronouns mean "become".
- ***mag-* relationship class** (p. 111):
  - *mag-* = "are", now (also future); *nag-* = "were"; *pag-* = participle;
  - ability forms *magka-*, *nagka-*, *pagka-*;
  - *-eV-* pluralises to three or more people (*magmaeanghod*).
- ***makig-/makipag-*** (p. 113): seeking mutuality with someone. The real forms are *nakig-*/*nakipag-*; the participle *pakig-*/*pakipag-* is rare.
- ***-in-* infix class** (p. 114), on noun or adjective roots:
  - "acting like" (*inamerikano*);
  - "speaking a language" (*inakeanon*, *binisaya'*);
  - derogatory (*linahog*).
- There is **no *-um-* class** on these pages. The book says the *-um-* conjugation has died out (p. 63; see §2).
- **ta-** (p. 115) is a relic prefix, as in *talikod*, *taliwan*, and inside *taga-*, *tag-*, *tig-*. **a(d)-** (p. 116) is a relic prefix making deictics into verbs (*ariya* "come here", *adto* "go") and is fossilised in *alima*, *alibangbang*, *aliputan*.

**Distributive *pang-*** (p. 106)

| time or object spread out | bakae → pamakae ("buy plenty"); hueog → panghueog ("drop plenty of things"); puso' → pamuso' ("break plenty of articles"); tinda(han) → panindahan ("go marketing") |

| goal in the stem | bunit → pamunit ("fish with a line"); kahoy → pangahoy ("gather wood"); isda' → pangisda' ("go fishing"); tabas → panabas ("tailor") |

## 5. Deictics as substantives, the time system, prepositional elements, quotatives, and idiomatic pronouns (pp. 161–168; context p. 160)

**Summary**
- A subclass of deictics works like nouns. They fill the topic, associate and referent slots, and they have four distances: near the speaker; near speaker and listener; near the listener only; far away.
- **Topic deictics** come in a standard form (ro-set / do-set), an enclitic form (always second in the clause, often + *nga*: *ro tawo ngara*), and three emphatic sets:
  - speaker-oriented *hara* set: demonstrating, pointing;
  - listener-oriented *mawraya* set: responding;
  - shared *rondaya* set: agreement or common knowledge.
- **Time system** (pp. 165–166):
  - *ku* + a time unit, date or event = completed past (*ku Lunes* "last Monday");
  - elsewhere, past time is "N + eon" (*daywang adlaw eon* "two days ago");
  - *it* = time within (*kahapon it hapon*);
  - *sa* = future (*sa Lunes* "on Monday").
- ***sa* is not itself a preposition.** Prepositional elements are used with *sa*/*kay* or a referent form (p. 166).
- **Quotatives** (p. 167):
  - *sin-* or *kun-* + an associative form = "X said";
  - *sin-* is the Kalibo-area form, and *kun-* is used around Ibajay in the north;
  - *makon*, *maton*, *mana* and *kuno* are particles.
- **Idiomatic pronouns** (p. 168):
  - a parenthetical *t-* form (*it* + akon, i.e. *takon/tang*, *timo/ting*, *tana/ta*) gives topic-less sentences;
  - **ka- + adjective root + ta + k-referent pronoun** gives exclamations ("You're so slow!").

**Topic, associate and referent deictics** (pp. 161–163)

| Meaning | ro-set | do-set | Enclitic | Speaker-oriented | Listener-oriented | Shared | Associate | Referent (long, short) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| this (near speaker) | raya | daya | ra | hara | mawraya | rondaya | kuraya | karaya, kara |
| that (near speaker and listener) | ruyon | duyon | ron | haron | mawruyon | ronduyon | kuruyon | karuyon, karon |
| that (near listener) | ran(h)a' | dan(h)a' | na | hana' | mawrana' | rondana' | kuran(h)a' | karan(h)a', kana' |
| that (far away) | rato | dato | to | hato | mawrato | rondato | kurato | karato, kato |

Alternates: hayra (hara), hayto (hato); mawra (mawraya), mawron (mawruyon [?]) (p. 162).

**Time with the oblique markers** (pp. 165–166)

| Marker | Time | Examples |
|---|---|---|

## 6. Basic function markers, the linker *nga*, and the interrogators (pp. 189–194; context p. 188)

**Summary**
- Function words carry grammatical rather than lexical meaning (ro/do, ku, it, sa). The book cross-references its earlier treatment: contractions p. 44, focus p. 77, verb classes p. 90 ff., causatives p. 102 f. (p. 189).
- ***mga*** pluralises the following noun phrase. ***ka*** is the enumerative after numbers and after *pila*. *mga* cannot be used with numbers. If *ka* is dropped, *nga* becomes obligatory (p. 190).
- ***nga*** is the linker, like an English relative "who / which / that". After a vowel, /h/, glottal stop or /n/ it shortens to ***-ng***. After other consonants it stays *nga* (p. 191).
- ***it*** **as a linker** (p. 191) appears in four places:
  - after negatives (*Ayaw it inom karon*);
  - in parenthetical pronouns (*tang*, *ting*);
  - to link a non-initial adverb (*Tuon it mayad*);
  - before complements (*Nasaktan ako it duro*).
- **Personal-name interrogators** follow the *si* paradigm: *sino*, *nanyo*, *anyo*, *kanyo*. *Sino ro imong ngaean?* is the true idiom. *Ano ro imong ngaean?* came in through foreign influence, and both are now accepted (p. 192).
- **Verbal interrogators**: *alin* (native) and *ano* (from Hiligaynon) mean "do what?". They take regular, stative and causative affixes (*Gaalin ka?*, *Pa-alin…?*) (p. 193).
- **Time interrogators**: *kan-o* = when, past; *hin-uno* = when, future. Both take a conditioned verb form, but *hin-uno* also takes future or subjunctive forms (p. 193).
- **Place**: *siin*, also "which (of two or more)". A conditioned (especially past) form stresses the importance of the place (p. 194).
- **Manner**: *haman* (mild "why"), *paano* [?] (forceful "why", contracted *pamisan* [?]), *basi* (northern "why"), *pa-ano*/*pa-alin* ("how").
- **Adjectival**: *pila*, *ikapila*, *mano*, *mauno*. All except *pila* are answered with adjectives (p. 194).

**Interrogators** (pp. 192–194)

| Type | Form | Meaning / slot |
|---|---|---|
| personal | sino | topic ("who") |
| | nanyo | associate, after the verb ("by whom") |
| | anyo | associate, before the noun ("whose") |
| | kanyo | referent ("to whom") |
| common noun | ano | topic ("what") |
| | nano | associate ("what") |
| | ano kuno | "what is it said?" (enclitic, with *kun-*) |
| verbal | alin, ano (Hiligaynon) | "do what?" |
| time | kan-o | "when" (past) |
| | hin-uno | "when" (future) |
| place | siin | "where"; "which" in comparisons |
| manner | haman | "why" (general, not forceful) |
| | paano [?] | "why" (great force, demands an answer) |
| | basi | "why" (northern Aklan) |
| | pa-ano, pa-alin | "how, in what way" |
| adjectival | pila | "how many / how much" |
| | ikapila | "what rank, what number" |
| | mano | "how much" (price) |
| | mauno | "how, of what quality" |
