## 1. Mood (pp. 66–69; context pp. 65, 70)

**Summary**
- Mood shows how the action is conceived. There are four moods: **simple**, **aptative (ability)**, **accidental**, and **imperative**.
- **Simple mood** is marked by the morpheme *ga-*, combined with the aspect morphemes *na-* (real) and *ma-* (unreal). It may go unexpressed, as with *-um-* and *ma-*.
- **Aptative mood**:
  - forms: *naka-* (real) and *maka-* (unreal) in the subjective focuses; *naha-* and *maha-* in the objective focuses;
  - meanings: "have an opportunity to" (*maka-*), "succeeded in" (*naka-*), and "can / could";
  - *naka-* + *eon* gives English "have/has …-ed";
  - *naka-* can also mean an unintentional or coincidental act.
- **Accidental mood** is marked by *hi-* (archaic *nahi-*, *mahi-*). The action happens purely by chance, often with *basi'* or *siin*.
- **Imperative mood** has a different morpheme for each focus. Actor-focus commands come in four strengths:
  - bare root (simple command);
  - *pag-* + root (very strong; not used to peers or superiors);
  - *-um-* (very common; the subject *ka* or *kamo* must be stated);
  - *mag-* (quite strong).
- **Polite commands** use the subjunctive form.
- **Negative commands** use *indi'* + the future-conditioned form (*mag-*), or *ayaw* + the past-conditioned form or + *it* + root.

**Mood morphemes** (p. 66; the simple-mood *ga* is underlined in the book)

|---|---|---|---|
| Simple | naga-, nag- | maga-, mag-, manog- [?], pag- | also -um-, ma-; ga- alone without aspect (gaadto) |
| Aptative | naka- (naha-) | maka- (maha-) | ka- in subjective focuses, ha- in objective (fn. 18) |
| Accidental | [nahi-] | [mahi-] | usually plain hi- |

## 2. Stative verbs (pp. 120–129)

**Summary**
- Stative verbs describe states (feelings, sensations, happenings).
- Unlike adjectives, they are not inflected for degree: there is no \*mas gutom or \*pinakagutom.
- Unlike regular verbs, their Actor Focus forms are defective. The *na-* form carries present or imperfective meaning (nagutom ako, "I am hungry").
- Their conjugations are peculiar to them; for example, the past-conditioned *gi-…-a* does not exist for regular verbs.
- There are five classes:
  - **I**: inner feelings (fullest conjugation);
  - **II**: involuntary personal states and happenings (aptative and happenstance forms only);
  - **III**: the same for inanimate subjects only (no *si*-type actor);
  - **IV**: transitive, with Object Focus forms;
  - **V**: referent forms in *-an*.
- **tag-** added to a root gives "feel like / might want to" (natagsaot ako, "I feel like dancing").
- Stative roots also form:
  - **ka- nouns** (kagutom, "hunger");
  - **maka- + -eV- adjectives** ("makes you …");
  - regular verbs with **kina-** ("…-ing and …-ing; deeply") or **ka-**.

| Imperfective | na---, (gi)na--- | — | — | na---, gina--- | — |
| Perfective | \*gin---, -in- | — | — | gin---, -in- | — |
| Future | (paga)---on | — | — | (paga)---on | — |

**Members**
- **Class I** (p. 121): -duyog ("be sleepy"), -gutom ("be hungry"), -ga-oy ("be tired"), -ohaw ("be thirsty"), -aslah [?] ("be oversatiated"), -kibot ("be shocked, startled"), -hidlaw ("be lonely; long for"), -mingaw ("be lonely; be depressed"), -naw-ay ("be disgusted, unhappy with"), -sueok [?] ("be famished"), -sum-oe ("be bored, tired of").
- **Class II** (p. 123):
  - internal states: -akig ("be angry"), -hadlok ("be afraid"), -ila' ("like; love"), -lipat ("forget"), -lipay ("be happy"), -sadla' [?] ("be sick and tired of"), -subo' ("be sad"), -tueog ("be asleep");
  - physical conditions: -duea' ("be lost"), -hueog ("fall down"), -matay ("be dead"), -tapus ("be finished"), -tawo ("be born"), -tumba ("fall over"), -ulihi ("be late"), -waea' ("disappear").
- **Class III** (p. 124): -buka' ("get opened", as of an oyster or lips), -bukas ("get opened", as of a door), -dayon ("go through, continue"), -gisi' ("get torn"), -human ("get finished"), -samad ("get broken"), -tabo' ("happen"), -wasak [?] ("get shattered"), -pusa' ("get broken into pieces").
- **Class IV** (p. 126):
  - harming: -bulag ("blind"), -eabo' ("wound"), -nina [?] ("cut, wound"), -patay ("kill"), -sakit ("harm, injure"), -puling ("blind temporarily with a foreign element");
  - other: -kita' ("see"), -duea' ("lose"), -human ("finish"), -pusa' ("break"), -samad ("ruin; break"), -tapus ("end").
- **Class V** (p. 127): -gutom, -ohaw, -hapdi' ("pain"), -sakit ("hurt"), -ayaw ("have enough of"), -hugod [?] ("be obliged to"), -tamad ("tired of"), -waea' ("disappear; lose").

**Stative roots in other parts of speech** (p. 129)
- ka- nouns: kagutom ("hunger"), kaga-oy ("tiredness"), kalipay ("joy"), kasakit ("pain"), kasubo' ("sorrow"), kasum-oe ("boredom"), kabueusgon ("satiation, fullness").
- maka- + -eV- adjectives: makabueusog ("filling"), makadueuyog ("soporific; makes one sleepy"), makahieilo' ("poisonous"; spelling from p. 171), makaoeohaw ("makes you thirsty"), makasaeakit ("painful; makes you sick"), makasueubo' ("makes you sad"), makataeapon ("contagious").
- Regular verbs with kina-: -kinamatay ("dying and dying"), -kinatueog ("sleep deeply"), -kinaulihi [?] ("be very late"), -kinasakit ("be gravely ill").
- Regular verbs with ka-: -kaila' ("court"), -katueog ("sleep"), -kasundo' ("be in agreement"), -kasayod ("know").

**Examples**
1. Nagutom ako. ("I'm hungry.") (p. 121)
2. Gina-oy imaw sa sautan. ("He was tired at the dance.") (p. 122)
3. Owa' imaw giohawa. ("He is not thirsty.") (p. 122)
4. Uminom ka eagi it tubi' agod indi' ikaw pagohawon sa daean. ("Drink some water now so that you won't be thirsty on the way.") (p. 122)
5. Hidlawon ako kimo kon hipanaw ka eon. ("I will miss you if you go away.") (p. 122)
6. Kan-o ikaw natawo? ("When were you born?") (p. 123)
7. Naakig ako sa akong igkampud. ("I'm angry with my cousin.") (p. 123)
8. Basi' hilipat ka sa pagdaea it sueat ngato. ("You might forget to bring that letter.") (p. 123)
9. Hamadla' [?] eon ako sa tawo ngaron. ("I'm already sick and tired of that person.") (p. 123)
10. Nagisi' ro saewae. ("The trousers got torn.") Accidental, stative. Compare Gingisi' nana ro saewae. ("He tore the trousers.") Purposeful, regular. (p. 124)
11. Indi' kita-on ro bukid kon madueom. ("The mountain cannot be seen if it is dark.") (p. 126)
12. Nasaktan ako it duro ku imong ginhambae. ("I was deeply hurt by what you said.") (p. 128)
13. Mahapdi-an ka sa nina ngara. ("This cut will cause you pain.") (p. 128)
14. Natagtangis ako. ("I feel like crying.") (p. 128)
15. Hipos anay, nagakatueog si Steve. ("Please be quiet; Steve is sleeping.") (p. 129)

## 3. Causative pa- (pp. 99–105)

**Summary**
- A verb's *quality* is one of three: general (unmarked), **causative** (*pa-* placed right before the root), or distributive (*pang-*).
- With *pa-*, the subject is a **causer** who has, lets, or makes someone else (the **agent**) do the action.
- Aklanon *pa-* does not show the causer's or agent's attitude; English "let / have / make" do. Use sugo ("command"), pilit ("force"), or paadto ("send") when the attitude matters.
- What *pa-* gives instead is free focus choice: on the causer, the agent, the goal, the instrument, or the location.
  - **Actor Focus** stresses the causer.
  - **Object Focus** stresses the agent (or the goal).
- An unfocused agent takes *sa*; an unfocused causer takes *ku*.
- Even intransitive roots gain Object Focus forms when the agent is emphasized.
- Idiomatically, *pa-* + a place or direction word means "go to / toward" (pauli', "go home").

**Root vs pa-** (p. 99)

| Root | pa- form |
|---|---|
| kaon ("eat") | pakaon ("feed") |
| kasayod ("know") | pasayod ("notify") |
| kita' ("see") | pakita' ("demonstrate") |
| daea ("bring") | padaea ("send") |
| bueog [?] ("cut") | pabueog [?] ("get a haircut") |
| dumdum ("think") | padumdum ("remind") |
| hueam ("borrow") | pahueam ("lend") |
| intra ("join") | paintra ("enter, have join") |
| obra ("make") | paobra ("have made") |

**Function markers in causative clauses** (pp. 102–103)

| Focus | ro/do (topic) | ku | it | sa |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Actor | causer | instrument/associate; often goal | goal, especially indefinite | agent; any referent |
| Instrument | instrument or conveyed object; often goal | causer; unfocused associate | goal if not in focus | agent; referents |
| Object | agent (or goal) | causer; nominal associate | goal when the agent is topic | referents; agent if not emphasized |
| Referent | emphasized referent (location, beneficiary) | causer or instrument | direct object | unfocused referents |

**Paradigm 2**, "Mother had Lito buy candy for Maria." (p. 104)

| Focus | Sentence |
|---|---|
| Actor (causer) | Nagpabakae si Nanay kay Lito it dulsi para kay Maria. |
| Instrument (goal) | Igpabakae [?] ni Nanay kay Lito ro dulsi para kay Maria. |
| Object (agent) | Ginpabakae ni Nanay si Lito it dulsi para kay Maria. |
| Referent (beneficiary) | Ginpabakean ni Nanay kay Lito it dulsi si Maria. |

**Idiomatic pa- for motion** (p. 105): -paeapit ("approach"), -paeayo' ("withdraw"), -pabanwa ("go to town"), -pabukid ("go to the hills"), -pailaya ("go inland"), -pailawod ("go seawards"), -pauli' ("go home"), -paKalibo ("go to Kalibo"), -pahimeno ("travel greatly").

**Examples**
1. Magaobra ako it baeay. ("I will build a house.") / Magapaobra ako it baeay. ("I will have a house built.") / Magapangobra ako it baeay. ("I will build several types of houses.") (p. 99)

4. Ginpalingkod nimo ro bisita? ("Did you have the visitor sit down?") (p. 102)
5. Si Roney nagpakanta sa anang mga igmanghod. ("Roney made his brothers sing.") (p. 102)
6. Palingkora ro bisita. ("Have the visitor sit down.") (p. 102)
7. Ro karne ginpakiwa' ku iná sa anang unga'. ("The mother made her child carve the meat.") (p. 102)
8. Paadtuna imaw riya. ("Let him come here." / "Have him come here.") (pp. 102, 105)
9. Ipatawag mo ro doktor. ("Have the doctor called.") (p. 105)
10. Ro Dyos owa' nagapabaya' sa tawong nagahingoto' [?]. ("God does not leave to himself the person who works hard.") (p. 105)
11. Pamat [?] owa' nimo ako pagpasudla sa andang Club? ("Why didn't you let me enter their Club?") (p. 105)

## 4. Adjectives (pp. 169–180; idiom from p. 168)

**Summary**
- Adjectives are descriptive or predicative. They have no focus, aspect, or tense, but they do inflect for **degree**.
- Most have a root form in *ma-* and no *na-* form. They follow qualifiers such as medyo, and link to nouns with *nga*.
- **Derivational sets**:
  - bare roots;
  - reduplicated roots ("slightly");
  - -in- ("has the character of");
  - -an / -on ("has the quality of"; often illness words);
  - -anon / -anan;
  - ma- (the largest set);
  - maka- + -eV- ("-ous, -some");
  - ma- + -in- + -on ("-ful");
  - ma- + -an;
  - maki- ("prone to");
  - paea- ("fond of");
  - pina- ("made to").
- **Degrees**: positive, diminutive (reduplication), relative (-ue- / mue-ma-: "a little more", usually comparing a thing with itself), intensive (ka- "very"), superlative (ka-…-an, the most idiomatic; pinaka- "considered the most"; or + gid).
- **Comparisons**:
  - equality: kamana ku / pareho ku, or kasing-; negatives iba ku [?], kabos ku, kueang ku;
  - scalar: mas, pa, reduplication + sa, archaic eabing; negative bukon it, or minus;
  - consequence: adjective + nga clause; negative with indi';
  - superlative within a group: ka-…-an / pinaka- + sa tanan.
- Personal names, pronouns, and deictics take Referent forms (kay, kimo, karon) instead of *sa*/*ku*.

## 5. Expanded noun phrases and the pronoun system (pp. 155–160; context pp. 154, 161)

**Summary**
- A marker binds everything after it into one noun phrase.
- **Possession**: an associate phrase can come *before* the noun (ana ku … nga + noun) or *after* it (noun + ku …). After the noun, *it* marks an indefinite possessor.
- **Linkers**: *nga* (shortened to -ng after vowels) works like English relative pronouns. "Ro tawo nga…" means "the person who…".
- **Spatial relators**: *sa* is vague about location ("at, in, into, by"), so a relator noun + *it* makes it precise (sa sueod it baeay, "inside the house").
- **Marker choice changes meaning**: Naila' ako *sa* imong asawa ("I like your wife") vs *it* imong asawa ("I want your wife").
- **Pronouns** never take markers, have separate singular and plural forms, and have one form per function. Orientation is speaker / speaker+listener / listener / other.
- Enclitic forms (ka, ta, ko, mo) must come second in the clause and precede all other enclitics.

**Possessive phrases** (p. 155)

| Possessor | Prepositive | Postpositive |
|---|---|---|
| Common noun, singular | ana ku … nga + N | N + ku … |
| Common noun, plural | anda ku mga … nga + N | N + ku mga … |
| Personal name, singular | ay … nga + N | N + ni … |
| Personal name, plural | anday … nga + N | N + nanday … |

**Spatial relators** (p. 156), all of the form *sa X it* + noun: sa sueod it ("inside"), sa kilid it ("at the side of"), sa idaeum it ("underneath"), sa atubang it ("in front of"), sa ibabaw it ("on top of"), sa pihak it ("on the other side of"), sa [?] it suba' ("at the edge of the river").

**Pronouns** (pp. 157–160)

| Orientation | Topic (sg / pl) | Associate, prepositive (+ nga) | Associate, postpositive | Associate, enclitic | Referent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Speaker | ako / kami | akon / amon | nakon / namon | ko / [?] | kakon / kamon |
| Speaker + listener | — / kita, ta | — / aton | — / naton | — / ta | — / katon [?] |
| Listener | ikaw, ka / kamo | imo / inyo | nimo / ninyo | mo / [?] | kimo / kinyo |
| Other | imaw / sanda | ana / anda | nana / nanda | na [?] / [?] | kana / kanda |

Notes on this table:
- The enclitic table on p. 160 is illegible in the scan. Only ko, mo, and ta are confirmed, by the examples.
- The Referent forms are "k- + prepositive set" (p. 160). katon and kana are reconstructed from that rule; kakon, kamon, kimo, kinyo, and kanda appear in examples.
- The postpositive set is the one used after negative, interrogative, and other prepositive particles.

**Topic deictics** (p. 161): raya / daya ("this", near the speaker); ruyon / duyon ("that", near listener and speaker); ran(h)a' / dan(h)a' ("that", near the listener); rato / dato ("that", far away). Enclitic forms: ra, ron, na, to.

**Examples**

## 6. Discourse particles (pp. 199–209)

**Summary**
- Particles add "colour" without changing grammatical function.
- **Enclitics** immediately follow the word they colour:
  - question and answer: baea, -a, man, abi, gang, ron;
  - time: eon, pa, rayon, anay, eagi;
  - quotative: kuno, makon, maton, mana;
  - limiting: eang, mismo, haeos, hingan;
  - emphatic: gid, ki, gali', ngani'.
- *eon* (real aspect: "already, now") and *pa* ("still, yet") never occur in the same clause.
- *man* is the all-purpose response particle; almost every answer carries it.
- **Prepositive particles** open the clause:
  - verbal: ta-o, abi/ambi, sige, pwede/sarang/mahimo;
  - modal: basi', siguro/sigurado, kunta', giato', sabon;
  - qualifying: syempre, matsa, maskin, medyo, manga.
- **Tag particles** close the clause: ano, ha, ho, ai, a.

**Enclitic particles** (pp. 199–204)

| Particle | Meaning (book) |
|---|---|
| baea | question emphasis: "indeed? really?" |
| -a | obligatory suffix on roots after siin / ano (adlawa, lataha) |
| man | general response: "also, too, so"; marks an answer or agreement |
| abi | excuse: "but, take it from me", giving a reason |
| gang (ugang) | reluctant or apologetic reply; from ugaling ("unfortunately"); colloquial |

- **Coordinating** conjunctions always stand *between* the elements they join. Those elements are of the same class (nouns with nouns, clauses with clauses).

## 8. Adverbs (pp. 181–187)

**Summary**
- Adverbs answer *when? where? how? how much?*. They can never be put in focus.
- Many adjective forms double as adverbs. An adverb is marked either by **initial position** or by the marker **it** before it. Compare the adjective, which is linked to its noun with *nga*:
  - Mayad nga manogobra imaw. ("He is a good worker.")
  - Nagaobra imaw it mayad. / Mayad imaw nagaobra. ("He works well.")
- True adverbs, such as kahapon, can stand anywhere without *it*.
- **tig-** + a number or quantity gives distributive manner ("two at a time").
- The four **deictics** follow the pronoun orientation. They have:
  - a basic form, used first in the clause;
  - a **postpositive** form (r-/d-), used elsewhere when *hay* is absent;
  - an **emphatic** form (rika-/dika-), for motion or demonstration.
- Deictics also express conversational and psychological distance. Si Marde *ngara* ("this Marde", approving) vs *ngaron / ngato* ("that Marde", disapproving).

**Time and manner words** (pp. 181, 184)
- Time: kaina ("earlier"), kabi-i ("last night"), kahapon ("yesterday"), hin-aga ("tomorrow"), hinduna' ("later on"), makaron ("now"), adlaw-adlaw ("daily"), gab-i-gab-i [?] ("nightly"), kato anay ("once upon a time"), temprano ("early"), ulihi ("late"), imaeaka' [?] ("seldom"), taeagsa ("rarely"), sa Lunes ("on Monday"), ku Huwebes ("last Thursday"), sa umabot nga bueoan ("in the coming month"), kaina nga aga [?] ("earlier this morning").
- Manner: mabakas ("fast"), mabaskog ("strongly"), madali ("quickly"), maeumo ("easily"), mahinay ("slowly"), malipay ("happily"), it mayad ("well"), it mahipos ("quietly"), it duro ("very much"), it sangkurot ("a little").
- tig-: tigsambato ("one at a time"), tigdaywa ("two at a time"), tigtatlo ("three by three"), tiglima ("five at a time"), tigsangkurot ("a little at a time").

## 9. Noun derivation (pp. 135–139; context pp. 134, 140)

**Summary**
- Noun affixes are derivational and keep the stress inside the root. Verb affixes pull the stress to the end: hampangan ("playground") vs the verb hampangán.
- **pag-** is the gerund of the actor-focus verb ("-ing"). **pagka-** names a fixed state.
- **pang-, panga-, pani(ng)-, panghi(ng)-** are gerunds of the instrument-focus verb.
- **Occupational nouns**:
  - maNV- (mang- + reduplication) for a full-time occupation;
  - manog- for a part-time doer;
  - archaic mag-CV- and maka-CV- for God;
  - -umV- for a regular doer;
  - inog- for the tool of a job;
  - pang- for a thing's established use.
- **Relationship nouns**: mag- (pairs; with -eV- for three or more), ig- (one specific relative), isigka- ("fellow").
- **ka- nouns**:
  - ka- alone: attained quality or role;
  - ka-…-an: abstract quality or a place;
  - ka-…-anan;
  - ka-…-on: states, matching the statives;
  - kina-: the culmination or essence;
  - kasi(ng)-: "from the same source".
- **pa- nouns** come from the causative; also paka-, pina-, paea- (habitual), paki-, pakig-.

## 10. Negatives and transformation products (pp. 195–198; context p. 194)

**Summary**
- **owa'** with verbs: with the past-conditioned form, past time; with the imperfective, present or habitual. Owa' + *it* means "do not have".
- **indi'** + the future-conditioned form gives future negation, and can also give a command.
- **ayaw** gives negative commands, with the past-conditioned form or with *it* + root.
- **bukon** negates adjectives and descriptive or identifying phrases, often contrasting with a topic.
- With eon: Owa' eon ("No more [left]"), Ayaw eon ("Not now", a strong "stop"), Bukon eon ("No longer [true]").
- **hay** fronts any element: the topic, a time word, a place. It is roughly "well…".
- **may** makes existential statements ("there is"), indefinites ("someone"), and possession. Possession is either *may* + thing + Topic possessor, or *may* + prepositive associate + *nga* + thing.

| Particle | Use | Verb form |
|---|---|---|
| owa' | past / present negation; "don't have" (+ it) | past conditioned (mag-, pag-…-a); imperfective |
| indi' | future negation; mild command | future conditioned (mag-, pag-…-on) |
| ayaw | negative command | past conditioned; or it + root |
| bukon (it) | negates adjectives, descriptions, identities | — |
| hay | fronts an element for emphasis | — |
| may | existential, indefinite, possession | — |
