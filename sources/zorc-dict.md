## IMPORTANT reliability caveats (read before using)

- This list was compiled **only through the WebFetch tool**, which summarises each 100,000-character chunk with a small model. The fetcher repeatedly reported that the OCR is heavily garbled (the special letter "e" is often OCR'd as "ae", "d", "6", etc.) and that its view of each chunk was truncated, so every chunk was only **partially** read. This is a **sample, not an exhaustive census**, of the dictionary.
- The page numbers are the fetcher's approximations from running page markers. Treat them as +/- several pages. The "section" is the alphabetical letter section.
- Glosses are the fetcher's paraphrases, not verbatim. Every entry **must be verified against the scanned PDF** before it goes into the app. Entries flagged (?) were flagged as OCR-doubtful by the fetcher. Several entries look implausible or mis-paired (flagged "pairing uncertain" in notes).
- The 0-100,000 chunk reached only the first A-entries; the A section proper (about pp. 43-69) was read in the 100,000-200,000 chunk, which mixed A and B material. The S-section (~pp. 323-356) and parts of H, K, G, and the whole T-W tail were only thinly read.
- Letters not clearly covered or only glimpsed: much of B (pp. 70-112), G-H gap near pp. 189-200 (partly covered), and most of S and T.

## Front matter: spelling and the special vowel (as reported by the fetcher; paraphrased, verify against the front matter of the PDF)

- Purpose: root-word dictionary for Aklanon speakers (standardising spelling, English idiom, Kalibo standard speech) and for foreign learners (translation, pronunciation, memorisation).
- Alphabetical order: sounds are merged; the vowels i/e and o/u are grouped. Foreign sounds are vernacularised for sorting (c/qu/cu sort with k; j with dy; ch with ts). Look-alike entries are ordered by stress first, then by glottal stop.
- **Special letter "e"**: used for a voiced velar fricative consonant and also for the vowel /e/. It is written as a consonant before a vowel (example given: *baeay*) and as a vowel next to a consonant (example given: *pwede*).
- k replaces c. Final /h/ is printed in parentheses, e.g. *tinda(h)*. Glottal stop is a hyphen mid-word and a mark (') word-finally, e.g. *baho'*. Stress is marked with an acute accent. Diphthongs are written with w or y. Foreign proper names keep original spelling.
- Entry format: root first, then derivations; a part-of-speech code (e.g. RV1-RV7 verb classes, ST1-ST5 stative classes, T/A/R pronoun sets, Tm/Rm markers, Qp question particle, Dp discourse particle, CV causative/other verb, Neg negative); etymology tags ([Sp], [Eng], [Tag], [Hil], [Ch], [Jap]); register tags ([vul], [stf], [gen], [slg], [sup]). The fold-out abbreviation key was not seen by the fetcher.
